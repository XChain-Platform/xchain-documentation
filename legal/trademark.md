Trademark is different: it protects the *name and brand* so users can trust that something called "XChain" is the real thing. You can run the software freely; you cannot use the Marks to brand your own version without permission.

## You may, without asking us

- **Use the Marks nominatively** to refer to the XChain Platform, e.g. "compatible with XChain," "built on XChain," "an XChain node operator," "works with the XChain protocol."
- **Redistribute the official, unmodified software** under its real name.
- Use the word "XChain" in **truthful, descriptive** statements about interoperability or support.
- Use the Marks in articles, tutorials, talks, and reviews.

The guiding rule: you may use the Marks to **describe** XChain truthfully. You may not use them in a way that implies your product **is** XChain or is **endorsed by** Dankest, LLC.

## You may NOT, without written permission

- **Name your own product, service, fork, company, or token "XChain"** or anything confusingly similar.
- **Brand a modified version** of the software with the Marks. If you change the code, you must distribute it under a **different name** (you keep the AGPL rights to the code, you just can't use our brand on your fork).
- Use the Marks (or look-alike logos/domains) in a way that **implies official status, endorsement, partnership, or affiliation** with Dankest, LLC when none exists.
- Register domain names, social media handles, app names, or company names containing "XChain" or confusingly similar terms.
- Use the Marks on merchandise for sale.

## Modified versions

If you distribute a modified version of XChain Platform:

- You **must** give it a distinct name and branding that is not confusingly similar to the Marks.
- You **may** truthfully state that it is "based on XChain Platform" or "a fork of XChain" (nominative use).
- You **may not** present it as the official XChain Platform.

This is the standard open-source pattern: the *code* is free (AGPL), the *brand* is protected, exactly how Firefox/Iceweasel and Red Hat/CentOS have always worked.
